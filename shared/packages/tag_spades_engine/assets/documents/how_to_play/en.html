How to Play Spades

Bid tricks with your partner, then make your bid.

The Goal

Finish with the highest score. Your team bids a number of tricks each hand, then tries to win at least that many. The game ends when a team reaches the target chosen in Settings; the highest score wins.

Teams and Dealing

Players 1 & 3 are partners, and players 2 & 4 are partners: you always sit across the table from your partner. Each player gets 13 cards. The deal passes to the left after every hand.

Bidding

Before play, each player bids the number of tricks (0–13) they expect to win. Your team's bid is the sum of both partners' bids.

Nil: instead of a number, you may bid Nil, betting that you will take no tricks at all. A made Nil earns your team a 100-point bonus; a broken Nil (you took a trick) costs your team 100 points. Your partner's numeric bid still counts separately.

Playing

  • The player left of the dealer leads the first trick. Follow the suit that was led if you can.
  • If you cannot follow suit, you may play any card, including a spade. Spades cannot be led until a spade has been played ("spades broken"), unless you hold only spades.
  • The highest spade played wins the trick; if no spades were played, the highest card of the led suit wins. The winner leads the next trick.

Scoring

After 13 tricks, add up each team's tricks. If your team took at least as many tricks as it bid, you score 10 points per bid trick, plus 1 point for each extra trick (a "bag"). If your team fell short, it loses 10 points for every trick it bid.

Bags carry over between hands. Every time your team's running bag count reaches 10, it loses 100 points and the count drops by 10.

Tips

  • Count your sure winners (high spades and aces) before you bid, and talk your bid over with how strong your hand feels, not just its size.
  • Watch your partner's bid: a high combined bid is riskier, so play carefully to avoid going set.
  • Use the Hint button for a suggested bid or card.
