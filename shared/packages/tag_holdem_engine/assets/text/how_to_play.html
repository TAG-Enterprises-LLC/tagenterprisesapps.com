T-G-Apps Holdem How to Play

Complete instructions for standard pass-and-play and human-only multi-phone Texas Hold’em.

Goal

Win chips by making the best five-card poker hand from your two private hole cards and the five community cards, or by making every other player fold before showdown.

Standard Game Setup

  1. Choose 2 to 6 players.
  2. Player 1 is always a Human, regardless of the name entered. Player 1 cannot be changed to a Robot.
  3. Players 2 through 6 may be Human or Robot. Enter a name for each Human; Robot seats receive robot names automatically.
  4. Review the Casino Table summary or open Settings to change the rules.
  5. Select Start Game for standard pass-and-play on one phone.

Reset to Default Settings restores the default setup. The app remembers the last-used setup and music choices.

Casino Table Settings

  • Game Type: No Limit, Pot Limit, or Fixed Limit.
  • Blinds: $1/$2, $1/$3, $2/$5, $5/$10, $10/$20, or $25/$50.
  • Ante: No ante, a $1/$2/$5/$10 Player ante, or a Big blind ante.
  • Starting Buy-In: $200, $500, $1,000, $2,000, or $5,000 for players without a saved bank.
  • Robot Action Speed: Fast (1 second), Normal (3 seconds), or Slow (5 seconds). This setting applies only to standard games because multi-phone games have no Robots.
  • Audio: Turn background music on or off and set its volume.

Settings selected on the Setup screen also supply the table rules when the host creates a multi-phone room.

Dealer, Blinds, and Antes

The dealer button moves after each hand. The next player posts the small blind and the following player posts the big blind. If selected, each player posts a Player ante, or the big blind posts the Big blind ante. The table shows dealer (D), small blind (SB), and big blind (BB) markers.

Cards and Betting Rounds

Each player receives two private hole cards. Betting proceeds through:

  1. Preflop: before community cards are dealt.
  2. Flop: three community cards.
  3. Turn: one additional community card.
  4. River: the fifth community card.
  5. Showdown: remaining hands are compared.

Folded players are skipped. Betting continues until every eligible player has acted and the current bet has been matched.

Player Actions

  • Check: pass when no bet is owed.
  • Call: match the amount currently owed.
  • Bet: open the wagering for the current round.
  • Raise: increase the current bet.
  • Fold: give up the hand and any chips already contributed.
  • All In: commit the available bank when permitted by the table structure.

Betting Controls

  • No Limit: select chip images to build a bet or raise, use Undo to remove the last chip, or choose All In. Press the Bet or Raise button to confirm the selected amount.
  • Pot Limit: use the same chip controls, but the maximum raise is limited by the pot. Max Bet selects the permitted maximum.
  • Fixed Limit: the app supplies the allowed Bet and Raise amount. The normal limit is used preflop and on the flop; the larger limit is used on the turn and river.

In standard pass-and-play, complete the action and then press Next Player. When a round is complete, the button advances to the dealer, next street, showdown, or hand recap. Robots act automatically after the configured delay.

Hint Button

Tap the green Hint button with purple lettering in either standard or multi-phone play. The chalkboard hint considers the current player, amount to call, pot, community cards, and stage of the hand.

Showdown, Recap, and Banks

If more than one player remains after the river, hole cards are revealed and the best five-card hand wins. If everyone but one player folds, that player wins immediately. The recap displays winners, results, and updated banks.

Players without enough chips to continue are removed from the current game and may begin a later game with a new bank. Named Human banks and game results are recorded for Games History. Select New Hand to continue after reviewing the recap.

Multi-Phone Requirements

  • A multi-phone room requires 2 to 6 Human players and an internet connection.
  • Player 1 is always a Human on the host phone.
  • Players 2 through 6 may be Humans sharing the host phone or Remote Humans using their own phones.
  • At least one seat must be a Remote Human.
  • Robot players are never allowed in multi-phone games.

Create a Multi-Phone Game

  1. Choose the desired table rules in Settings, then select Multi-Phone Play.
  2. Select Create Game and choose 2 to 6 seats.
  3. Enter each player’s name. Player 1 remains on this phone. For every other seat, choose This phone or Remote.
  4. Press Create Game. The lobby displays a six-character room code that can be copied and shared.
  5. Keep the host in the lobby while Remote Humans join.

Join a Multi-Phone Game

  1. On the joining phone, select Multi-Phone Play, then Join Game.
  2. Enter the host’s complete six-character room code and your player name.
  3. Press Join Game. The phone claims the next available Remote Human seat.
  4. In the lobby, press I Am Ready. Press Not Ready if you need to pause before the game starts.

Multi-Phone Lobby

The lobby shows every seat, whether it is on the host phone or Remote, and each Remote Human’s connection and readiness status. The host can start only after all required Remote Humans have joined, are online, and are ready. Selecting Back asks the host to close the room for everyone or asks a Remote Human to leave the room.

Playing on Multiple Phones

  • Every phone displays the same dealer, board, pot, current bet, player banks, actions, and hand progress.
  • The active seat is highlighted. Only the phone controlling that seat receives its action controls.
  • A phone can read only the private hole cards for the seat or seats it controls. Private cards are shown when that seat acts and are revealed publicly only when the game reaches showdown.
  • Use Fold, Check, Call, All In, or the chip-based Bet/Raise controls. Other phones display a waiting message until the active player acts.
  • The server shuffles and deals, validates turn order and wager limits, advances the streets, creates side pots when necessary, evaluates hands, and synchronizes the recap.
  • If a Remote Human disconnects during play, the server may fold that disconnected seat so the remaining players can continue.
  • After the recap, only the host can press New Hand; all other phones wait for the host.
  • The game ends when fewer than two players have chips remaining.

Multi-Phone Privacy and Security

Firebase assigns each phone an anonymous game identity; players do not create an account or enter an email address. The server controls the deck, private cards, bets, and results. Room data is limited to room members, and each phone’s private-card record is limited to that phone’s Firebase identity.
